HOW A PHONE GETS IN

1. Turn on Phone remote in Setup (screen 14).
2. Scan the QR code shown there and type the PIN.
3. The phone appears on the laptop as “waiting”.
4. Someone at the laptop lets it in, as Full control or View only.
A phone that has been let in once reconnects by itself. New phones can only be let in from Edit mode, so nobody in the room can join during the show. No internet is needed.

Left: switching to Show mode runs a check and stops you if something would go wrong on stage. 8New song

Drop in the stems exported from your DAW, plus the song's MIDI file if you have one. The app guesses each part's role from its file name and reads the tempo, sections and pedal changes from the MIDI file. Anything it isn't sure of is in amber for you to set. The files are converted once, now, so the song loads fast on stage.

10Mixer

Two kinds of level, chosen with “Levels for” at the top. This song (first frame) is how loud each part is in this song, for everyone. Choosing a person or the PA (second frame) shows that output's own mix: how much of each part they get, in every song. So a part that is too quiet in one song is fixed in the first, and a drummer who wants more click is fixed in the second. The panel on the right shows the loudest moment each output will reach; one that would clip turns red and names the bar.

11Setup: audio and routing

A rig is one set of real hardware: the tour rig, the rehearsal room, headphones at home. Give each output a name and a label: In-ears only feeds someone's ears, PA may reach front of house, Timecode carries timecode only. Each column is that output's mix. Click a cell to send a role there and drag to set how loud; the number is in dB. Here the drummer has the click 3 dB up and no percussion, and the PA has every backing part and no click. A dash means it isn't allowed: click and cues can never go to a PA output.

12Setup: MIDI devices and controls

Top: each pedalboard has a name, a port and a channel on this rig, MIDI clock on or off, a timing nudge (so a slow-switching pedal gets its change a little early), and a list of patch names. Test sends its current patch. Bottom: what triggers each action. Every action has a key; click Learn and press a pedal or pad to add one. MIDI pedals are the safest choice on stage, because they work even when the keyboard is elsewhere.

15Snapshots

A safety net for edits made at the venue. A snapshot of every song, setlist and setting is taken each time the band folder is opened, and you can save your own. Going back to one is a single click, and the current state is saved first, so even that can be undone. Each snapshot lists what has changed since.
